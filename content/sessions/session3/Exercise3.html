Intro

Working with an unfamiliar tool creates a common ground: neither participant knows exactly what the tool can do, so both have to discover its possibilities through experimentation. The tool becomes an active part of the collaboration, influencing the forms, textures, and limitations of what can be made.

As Ivo Brouwer puts it: “... if you put yourself in an uncomfortable position, then something interesting will happen.” We look at the questions: What happens if you introduce an unknown variable into the collaboration? And how can discovering something new together shape what you both create?

Variable: Tool
Tools: Grid paper, Acrylic paint, (Round) sponge/
any unfamiliar tools you would like to experiment with
Time: 2+ hours
Fun parameter:
Warm-up Task: 10 minutes
Main Exercise: 2 hours
Instructions

Warm-up Task: Sabotage

10 mins

Step 1: One participant holds and moves the paper while the other holds the pen and draws.

Step 2: Switch roles after 5 minutes and repeat.

Do not try to create specific letters. Focus on how the movement of one person affects the lines made by the other.

Main Exercise: Tool to Type

2 hours

Step 1: Each participant explores the tools and acrylic paint independently for 10–15 minutes.

Step 2: In the next 15 minutes, use the tool to explore different letterforms. Focus on discovering how the characteristics of the tool can become part of the letters.

Step 3: Look at your experiments together. Discuss what kind of letterforms you want to develop and how you want to use which tool.

Step 4: Divide the alphabet between the two participants. Each person develops the letters independently, using the common framework you agreed on. If necessary, adjust the rules together during the process.*

*Talk to each other whenever you feel the need to. Discuss individual letterforms, exchange ideas, or ask for feedback. Use the conversation to support each other and shape the alphabet together – whether that means creating a coherent system or embracing the differences between your letters.
