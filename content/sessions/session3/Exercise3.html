Intro

Sharing a creative process means giving up total authority over the outcome. Even if each participant has a clear role, their actions constantly overlap and disrupt one another, whether by design or by accident. By letting go of control, we allow unexpected outcomes to happen.

In this working session, we will put this tension into practice. Using physical resistance and deliberate sabotage, the exercises ask the following questions: And how can we assume a position of control while surrendering the outcome to the process? And how can two people create something that is impossible for a single person to make?

Variable: Control
Tools: Blank paper, Brush or Pen
Number of sheets: 78 sheets
Time: 1.5-2 hours
Warm-up Task: 30 minutes
Main Exercise: 1 hour
Instructions

Look at the (uppercase) alphabet and list the essential traits that make each letter recognizable (for instance, an "A" needs at least two diagonal strokes meeting at the top).

Warm-up Task: From A to Z, From Z to A

30 mins

Step 1: Each person draws a letter on a different sheet. One person draws forward from A to Z, while the other draws backward from Z to A at the same time.

Step 2: As you draw on your own sheets, use the other hand to hold each other's drawing arms and control their direction of drawing to see what forms emerge from the physical tension.

Main exercise: Pen vs. Paper

1 hour

Step 1: Assign roles: one participant draws continuously, while the other moves the paper beneath the pen.

Step 2: Draw through the alphabet, letter by letter. Do not lift the brush/pen until you finish a letter. Once you feel its essential traits are met, lift the drawing tool and move on to the next.

Step 3: Switch roles and repeat the entire process for a second round so both participants can experience both roles.

Step 4: Gather all the drawings and select the most convincing version of each letter to complete your alphabet.
