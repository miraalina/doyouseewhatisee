Intro

We often hear "Too many cooks spoil the broth." But what if the cooks mix such wild, clashing ingredients that the result becomes so grotesque it turns back into something fascinating? Conflict is built into shared creation, and while compromise is the safe path, it isn't the only one.

In the previous session, we left decision-making to chance. In this working session, we will make the deliberate choice to embrace total inclusion. No edits, no eliminations. Throughout the two exercises, we question: Is total inclusion an alternative to compromise? And what does an outcome look like when it absorbs every single opinion?

Variable: Compromise
Tools: Pen, Paper, and Dot grid paper
Fun parameter: 6/6
Number of sheets: At least 52 sheets
Time: 3-4 hours
Warm-up Task: 1.5 hours
Main Exercise: 1.5 hours
Instructions

Warm-up Task: More is More

1.5 hours

Step 1: Work through the alphabet in four distinct stages in the following order:

Stage 1: B, E, G, K, R, M

Stage 2: A, F, N, P, S, W

Stage 3: D, J, Q, X, Y, Z

Stage 4: C, H, I, L, O, T, V

Step 2: Participants alternate turns. On your turn, apply exactly ONE modification to the current letterform. In each Stage, you are allowed to make the following modifications:

Stage 1: Skeleton & Proportions (B, E, G, K, R, M)

Allowed: Add, remove, or reposition structural elements. Alter scale, stroke length, width, height, or slant.

Forbidden: No stroke weight changes, line textures, or decorative additions.

Stage 2: Stroke & Weight (A, F, N, P, S, W)

Allowed: All Group 1 rules + alter stroke thickness or stroke path and style (straight, curved, broken, segmented).

Forbidden: No ornamental or decorative additions.

Stage 3: Ornamentation (D, J, Q, X, Y, Z)

Allowed: Add decorative elements, serifs, flourishes, inline accents, or secondary marks.

Forbidden: No alterations to the underlying structural anatomy or base stroke paths.

Stage 4: Full System Expansion (C, H, I, L, O, T, V)

Combine all accumulated rules from Stage 1–3 to construct the remaining letterforms into a complete, unified system.

Step 3: The next participant draws the following letter in the sequence, carrying over all accumulated modifications from previous turns and adding their own single modification. Repeat until all letters have been drawn.

Main Exercise: Type Chessboard

1.5 hours

Step 1: Set up a dot grid where every node is assigned an (X,Y) coordinate.

Step 2: Assign rules to specific coordinates. Decide what happens when a stroke lands on a certain coordinate (e.g., How many grid units must a line span? What is the minimum or maximum step count? In which direction must it move? Does reaching a point force the stroke to end?).

Step 3: Assign rules to the drawing process. Decide how the exercise proceeds (e.g., In what order do participants draw the alphabet? Where should each letter start? Does every letter start at the same coordinate, or does the next letter begin at the endpoint of the previous one? When and how should the drawing end? Should there be a time limit?).

Step 4: Assign any other rule to the exercise if you want to.

Step 5: Draw the alphabet strictly according to the rules you set. Don't overthink it, let the rules dictate the forms.
