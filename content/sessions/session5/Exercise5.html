Intro

In a collaboration, making decisions can be one of the most difficult parts of working together. Different ideas, preferences, and expectations have to be negotiated, and finding a solution that everyone agrees on often means compromising.

In this session, neither participant is allowed to decide. Instead, the decision is handed over to a third party: the tools. The participants can no longer negotiate individual outcomes – they can only respond to what the system gives them.

By taking decision-making out of the hands of the collaborators, we explore: What if we remove the need to make decisions altogether? Could not making decisions be an alternative to compromise in collaboration? And what remains of the collaborators’ individual input when the decisions shaping the letterforms are made by chance?

Variable: Decision
Tools: Pen, Grid paper, Dice, Coin, Spin Wheel
Number of sheets: 52 sheets
Time: 2 hours

Instructions

Warm-up Task: The Dice decides

30 min

Step 1: Flip a coin to decide who starts.

Step 2: Create a wheel with all letters from A–Z or a–z.

Step 3: Spin the wheel to randomly select a letter.

Step 4: Roll the dice to determine how many strokes, from 1–6, the letter should consist of.

Step 5: Draw the selected letter using the number of strokes determined by the dice. You decide what counts as one stroke.

Step 6: Repeat the process, alternating between participants.

Main Exercise: No Decisions

1.5 hours

Together, define the basic rules of the letter system. Use the decision wheel for decisions with more than 6 possible options, the dice for decisions with 1–6 options, and the coin for decisions with 2 options. Neither participant can make a decision themselves.

Step 1: Set up the following decisions:

Use the coin to decide whether

you work with uppercase or lowercase letters.

the shapes making up the letters should touch or not touch.

all shapes should have the same size or random sizes. (If the shapes have the same size, roll the dice to determine their size on the grid. For example, a roll of 3 means that each shape occupies a 3 × 3 square area)

If you want to introduce additional parameters, make the decision using the appropriate tool.

Step 2: Set up two wheels: one with the letters from A–Z and one with the geometric shapes you want to work with (e.g. square, rectangle, circle, triangle).

Step 3: Start building the letters. Flip a coin to decide who starts.

Step 4: Spin the wheels to randomly select a letter and determine which geometric shape will be used.

Step 5: Roll the dice to determine how many shapes the letter should consist of.

Step 6: Construct the selected letter using exactly the number and type of shapes determined by the rules. For example: D – Circle – 4 shapes.

Step 7: Repeat the process, alternating between participants, until the alphabet is complete.
