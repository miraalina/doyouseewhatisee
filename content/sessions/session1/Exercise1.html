Intro

For us, this was the final session of our project – yet we decided it serves as a great starting point for a collaboration. What does a collaboration actually need? What common ground do both people naturally bring to the table right from the start in terms of type? Their own personal handwriting.

By following the movement of the other person's pen, we learn to understand their individual type style. In the next step, both handwritings are individually fused. This session is more about getting to know each other and our way of thinking in the field of type, forming a first attempt at how to create something collaboratively yet individually.

This exercise addresses key questions: To what extent can observing someone's pen stroke help us understand their thought process? And what does a script look like when it fuses two distinct handwritings?

Variable: Personality
Tools: Pen, transparent paper
Number of sheets: 6-8
Time: 1.5 hours
Fun parameter:
Instructions

Step 1: On a sheet of transparent paper, draw guidelines using a ruler at a comfortable writing size (not too small). Replicate these guidelines onto all other transparent sheets.

Step 2: Each person writes on transparent paper:

  • The alphabet (a–z), lowercase and uppercase (each on a separate sheet)
  • Numbers

Step 3: Place two corresponding sheets on top of each other (e.g., lowercase + lowercase). Place a third transparent sheet on top to create a hybrid layer where both handwritings are fused into one. Each participant does this for both lowercase and uppercase letters, resulting in two fused versions each.

Approaches to try:

Frankenstein Method: Mix distinct elements from each letter.

In-Between Method: Blend the characteristics of both styles into a single, intermediate letterform.

Step 4: Look at the results together to see which letters turned out identical. Whenever you have two different versions of a letter, keep both as alternate styles.
