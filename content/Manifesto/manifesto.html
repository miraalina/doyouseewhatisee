Manifesto of Collaboration

A collaboration is when two or more people work together on a single subject,
the same interest, holding mutual respect for each other, setting clear expectations of
what they do and want to achieve, and always meeting each other at eye level.

Article 1

Form a collaboration where parties complement each other, rather than working with people who are similar to you.

Article 2

Have a shared understanding of how you want to work together and ensure you have similar expectations and requirements for your work and yourselves while working.

Article 3

Be transparent about and document the amount of work and different responsibilities of each partner within the collaboration.

Article 4

Trust the process; be flexible with the span and result of the collaboration.

Article 5

Create a space where you can communicate your ideas, doubts, needs, and opinions clearly with your partner.

Article 6

Don't settle for smooth, easy communication; use the tension of miscommunication, contrasting opinions, and deliberate friction to your advantage.

Article 7

Don't settle disagreements with compromises, use them as common ground to iterate, dig deeper, rethink, and come up with better ideas. A compromise isn't about everyone having to give something up – it's about finding a solution that both sides can be happy with or maybe realizing it's about being aware that someone might be right. Let's get rid of the negative connotation around the word "compromise."

Article 8

Allow yourself to lose total authority and trust each other to own distinct responsibilities based on your complementary strengths.

Article 9

Take care of your interpersonal relationship; show up for each other, respect each other's personal circumstances and resources such as time, social capacity, concentration, energy.

Article 10

Credit everyone involved in a collaboration, or make the crediting itself a collaboration.

Article 11

Maintain your personal life and physical health.

Article 12

Have fun.
